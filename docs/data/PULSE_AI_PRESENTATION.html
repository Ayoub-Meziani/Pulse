The idea

Data flows to the doctor; the AI helps them see earlier

1 · Measurements

  • Blood-pressure cuff readings
  • Ring / bracelet: heart rate, HRV, SpO2, skin temperature
  • Symptoms, lifestyle, medication doses
→

2 · Secure database

  • Supabase (Postgres 17) in the cloud
  • Every row checked on the way in
  • Each role sees only what it may see
→

3 · AI jobs

  • Tomorrow's BP estimate
  • Ring night monitor
  • Live early warning (every minute)
  • Weekly doctor report
→

4 · Doctor

  • Sees estimates, flags, weekly report
  • Reviews flags
  • Approves each patient's personal range

Rule we never break: the AI only informs the doctor. Alerts come from fixed medical rules in the backend; the AI never raises an alarm, diagnoses, or touches medication, and patients see no AI output.

Data

Simulated where no real data exists, real where it does

Simulated (always labelled)

  • Demo patients: 12 fictional patients with stories (sleep apnea, COPD, atrial fibrillation, rising BP)
  • Training cohort: 200 patients × 90 days. Day-to-day BP variation matched to a real home-BP series (variation 10.0 vs 10.0 mmHg)
  • Unseen test cohort: 100 new patients, used only for the 30-day test
  • Ring data: built from published values (normal ranges, apnea, COPD)
  • Live nights: 700 + 700 minute-by-minute nights with situations (oxygen falling, fever, bad sleep, loose ring…)

Real public data (statistics only, never uploaded)

DatasetUsed for
NHANES 2017–2020 (CDC, US adults)population pulse / BP bands; risk model training
NHANES 2021–2023external test on new real people
Mendeley home BP (1 patient, 128 readings)made the simulator realistic
PhysioNet Apnea-ECG (8 real nights)checked the ring oxygen rule

No open dataset has daily home BP for many patients. That is why the BP models are trained on calibrated simulation.

Next-day blood pressure · mbp-v1.1

Beats the simple methods on every one of 30 test days

Systolic error, mean absolute (mmHg); lower is better. 2,271 patient-days, 2–31 March, 100 patients no model had seen.

  • Day-by-day test: each day, the frozen model sees only earlier days, exactly as in real use
  • Lower error than "same as yesterday" on 30 / 30 days, and than the 7-day mean on 28 / 30
  • Diastolic error 4.56 mmHg (7-day mean 4.83)
  • The 90 % range contained the true value 90.7 % of the time
  • Shown to the doctor as "model estimate (synthetic training data)"

Synthetic test patients. Real accuracy needs consented daily home readings from PULSE users.

Ring night monitor · ring-v1.1

The patient's own normal finds what fixed limits miss

Planted events found out of 14 (30-day test, 2,703 simulated nights). Other flags per patient-week: limits only 1.12, IsolationForest 0.08, personal normal 0.22.

  • Three checks each night: medical limits (SpO2 < 94 %, resting heart rate > 100), the patient's own 28-day normal, and an anomaly detector
  • Conditions change the rules: COPD → SpO2 limit 88 %; atrial fibrillation → HRV ignored
  • Real check (PhysioNet): the oxygen rule flagged 4 / 4 real sleep-apnea nights and 0 / 3 healthy nights
  • Flags go to the doctor to confirm or dismiss; they never create alerts

Events were planted in simulated data. The real check is 8 nights from a sleep-lab oximeter, not a ring.

Honesty about real data

When a model was too weak on real people, we hid it

wrisk-v1 on real NHANES data

  • Trained on 2,309 real treated US adults: can risk factors tell whose BP is uncontrolled?
  • Tested on a later survey it never saw (2021–2023, 1,851 people)
  • AUROC 0.596 vs 0.565 for age + sex alone: stable, but too weak
  • Not shown to doctors, because a near-random score would mislead

Lesson: risk factors can't tell whose BP is controlled. Control has to be measured, which is exactly what PULSE does.

Safety by design

Designed so the AI never acts on its own

  • Advisory only: AI output goes to the doctor, never to the patient (enforced in the database)
  • Fixed rules decide alerts in the backend: BP ≥ 180/120 and red-flag symptoms; ring rule R-RING-1 proposed
  • Emergency number 14 (Protection civile); never dialled in tests or the demo
  • No medication advice anywhere
  • Every simulated row is labelled; real datasets used as statistics only, under their licences
  • Every model has a model card: data, method, results, limits

Likely questions, short answers

QuestionAnswer
Is this real patient data?No. All patient data is simulated and labelled. Real public data was used to make the simulator realistic and to check the rules.
How accurate is it?On 100 unseen simulated patients, next-day systolic error is 7.9 mmHg, better than simple methods on every one of 30 days. Real accuracy needs consented PULSE data.
Can the AI raise an alarm?No. Alerts come from fixed medical rules in the backend. The AI only informs the doctor; patients never see it.
Why not deep learning?Little data, and doctors need to understand the output. Small models beat simple baselines and are easy to explain.
You have no ring. How do you know it works?Ring data is simulated from published values, and the oxygen rule was checked on 8 real sleep-lab nights (4/4 apnea flagged, 0/3 healthy).
Did any model fail?Yes: the real-data risk model (AUROC 0.60) was too weak, so we hid it. A first live forecaster never warned; we replaced it.
What happens next?Real device, consented data, re-test every model on real people, clinician review of the pending thresholds, drift monitoring.
